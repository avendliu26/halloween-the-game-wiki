## Quick Answer

Michael Myers is the lone killer in 1v4 multiplayer. His job is to stalk and kill residents across Haddonfield, including NPC residents and marked Special Targets — not only the four player-controlled Civilians. Killer Sense and Shape Jump are always part of his loadout; he also selects three additional abilities and a starting weapon.

The practical loop is simple: locate activity, stalk targets, create darkness, Shape Jump into position, and choose efficient kills instead of long open-street chases. Stalking and executions build Bloodthirst, which strengthens Michael during the match.

Killer Sense and Shape Jump are the two fixed starting traits described by IllFonic. Reality Tear, Blackout, and Detection Pulse are examples of the additional abilities that can fill Michael's three selectable slots. Higher Killer progression unlocks more abilities and customization choices.

## Killer Sense and Stalk

Activate Killer Sense to look through Michael's mask and locate nearby potential victims. When you hold a target in view, the Stalk meter builds on that individual. Stalk helps track the target and unlocks increasingly effective execution opportunities.

Use Killer Sense at the start while Shape Jump is unavailable, after losing a target inside a building, or when searching for movement among detection markers. Community players note that stationary detections may be televisions or radios while moving detections are more likely to be residents; treat that as a current observation, not an official icon glossary.

Stalk before committing to a grab when possible. Community testing also reports that wounded or frightened targets accumulate Stalk more quickly. Exact multipliers are not officially published, so watch the target's in-game meter rather than relying on a permanent numeric formula.

## Shape Jump

Shape Jump is Michael's defining movement ability. It lets him travel unseen at increased speed, move through parts of the environment, and use Shape Dash to close distance. It can remain active while you reposition; the cooldown begins when you return to normal form.

Normal entry and exit depend on darkness and whether residents can see Michael. The older official Shape Jump article phrases the restriction as both direct light and Civilian line of sight, while the newer multiplayer overview describes darkness or being outside a resident's sight. In current play, the safest rule is to seek real darkness and break line of sight before entering or leaving.

Create routes by switching off indoor lights and damaging exterior power boxes. Plan your exit before activating Shape Jump: a lit street or a group staring toward your approach can leave you unable to emerge where you intended.

### Shape Jump cooldown

Community-tested reports consistently describe the normal Shape Jump cooldown as approximately 5 seconds after Michael exits. This number is not in IllFonic's public documentation and may change with Bloodthirst, abilities, or future balance patches.

Players have also reported Shape Jump remaining unavailable for minutes even in darkness. That is inconsistent with the normal short recharge and appears to be a launch-version bug. Recheck light, line of sight, and the on-screen timer first; if the lockout persists, do not mistake it for a hidden intended cooldown.

## Reality Tear

Reality Tear is a long-cooldown answer to Shape Jump's normal restrictions. Community descriptions report that it lets Michael enter or exit Shape Jump while observed or in light, with a nearby stagger that creates an ambush opportunity. This makes it useful against grouped Civilians, defended exits, or targets who remain in well-lit streets.

Current community-tested timing places Reality Tear at approximately 3½–4 minutes between uses. Reports vary between roughly three and four minutes, so this is a range, not an official frame-accurate value. Bloodthirst or future balance changes may also affect what a player sees in a specific match.

Do not spend Reality Tear simply to cross an empty map. Save it for a target who is denying normal Shape Jump, a coordinated group, an urgent escape defense, or recovery from a bad position. Because the cooldown is measured in minutes, an unproductive use removes a major control tool for a significant part of the match.

## Blackout and Detection Pulse

Blackout supports Michael's map-control game by disrupting light and creating more favorable darkness. Use it around buildings, resident groups, or active objectives where ordinary light switches and power-box damage are too slow or exposed.

Detection Pulse supports target acquisition. Patch 1.0.1 increased its effectiveness for locating residents in hiding spots, making it more useful when warned residents disappear from obvious paths. IllFonic's public progression article names the ability but does not list a numeric radius, duration, or cooldown.

These selectable abilities compete for one of three loadout positions. Choose tools that solve your actual problem: finding hidden residents, controlling light, or bypassing Shape Jump restrictions. There is not enough official numeric data for a universal best build across every map and playstyle.

## Bloodthirst and Executions

Michael builds Bloodthirst by playing like a methodical killer: stalking, frightening, injuring, and executing victims. Community players report that executions and fully stalked kills provide stronger progress than relying only on quick melee damage. The current in-game meter is the authoritative indicator because official patch notes do not publish the complete point formula.

Higher Bloodthirst improves Michael's pressure and can influence ability recovery. This is another reason not to spend the entire opening chasing a single healthy Civilian in the street. Build momentum on residents, Special Targets, and achievable executions, then use the stronger state to contest organized players.

## Practical Michael Gameplay Loop

1. Open with Killer Sense and identify nearby resident activity.
2. Stalk a target while Shape Jump becomes available.
3. Kill lights or break power near the route you intend to use.
4. Shape Jump ahead of the target rather than following directly behind.
5. Leave the ability in cover, attack or grab, then reassess the town objective.
6. Break away from long player chases and return to residents or Special Targets.
7. Hold Reality Tear or another long-cooldown ability for a defended target or urgent escape.
8. Use noise from police calls and escape activations to find concentrated Civilian activity.

## Patch 1.0.1 Changes

The official launch post includes Patch 1.0.1. Relevant changes increased Detection Pulse effectiveness against residents in hiding spots, improved resident pathing into escapes, improved police NPC pathing, repaired crossplay party-follow behavior, and fixed additional challenge or achievement tracking problems.

The notes do not publish a Shape Jump or Reality Tear balance change. Keep the official patch facts separate from community cooldown measurements and launch-bug reports.
