Halloween: The Game Perk Cards modify Civilian play. Complete matches to earn Perk Points, spend points to roll cards, then equip owned cards in a Civilian Perk Deck. Spending more improves higher-rarity odds, while upgrading an owned card boosts it temporarily for a set number of matches.

## Points, Rolls, and Rarity

The official [progression overview](https://halloweengame.com/news/progression-customization-overview/) describes higher spending on a roll as improving the chance of higher rarity—not guaranteeing a particular card. Owned cards can also receive a points-funded upgrade that lasts for a limited number of matches.

Before spending, compare what you actually need:

| Decision | Useful question |
| --- | --- |
| Roll for more cards | Does my collection lack effects that fit my usual job? |
| Spend more on a roll | Am I comfortable paying more for better odds without a guaranteed result? |
| Temporarily upgrade | Will I play enough relevant matches to use the limited boost? |
| Change a deck | Are the cards I already own better suited to another Civilian or objective? |

Do not mistake an upgrade's remaining duration for permanent card progression. Check its current cost and match counter in the game before confirming. Exact odds, price brackets, and universal upgrade durations are not established by the cited overview.

## Constructing a Useful Deck

Start with the job you want to perform: warn residents, work an escape, support another player, or survive long enough to finish an objective. Then read each owned card's trigger and effect. A large-looking bonus is wasted if you rarely meet its condition.

A practical deck-building pass:

1. Choose the Civilian you will actually use.
2. Read that character's traits and starting item separately from the cards.
3. Select effects that support the tasks you perform most often.
4. Check the deck is equipped on that loadout, not merely saved.
5. After several matches, replace effects you never used before spending more points.

The current source does not establish a fixed deck-slot count or all slot-unlock milestones. Use the visible deck screen as the authority for capacity. Do not import another horror game's three- or four-perk loadout rules.

## Traits, Starting Items, and Cards

A character trait is part of the Civilian's identity. A starting item changes the opening inventory. A perk belongs to the card/deck system. Compare all three when evaluating a build, but do not describe one as unlocking the others without checking its actual requirement.

For example, a repair-focused setup should be evaluated around access to useful tools and the jobs you can reach safely—not just an attractive card rarity. A resident-focused setup should be judged on completed warnings and escorts, not only personal escapes. These are build-evaluation examples, not named meta decks.

See [Characters](/characters) for the four stat categories and [character unlocks](/guides/how-to-unlock-characters) for the difference between Profile and Civilian progress.

## Michael and Prestige

The official deck system is described for Civilians. Michael instead has his own traits, weapons, and selected abilities; do not transplant a Civilian perk recommendation into a killer loadout. Use the [Michael guide](/characters/michael-myers).

Prestige is separate from temporary card upgrades. Available official text does not fully document what every prestige category resets or retains, so this page does not promise that a collection, currency balance, or loadout is unaffected. Read the live confirmation before committing.
