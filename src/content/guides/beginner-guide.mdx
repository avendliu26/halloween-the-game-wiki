## Quick Answer

Start with Story Mode if you want to learn Michael Myers, then play a few Civilian matches with one simple priority: find and warn residents before trying to solve everything alone. Michael should stalk the town and control darkness; Civilians should divide rescue, police, and escape work while staying close enough to help one another.

Do not leave when your Civilian dies. Spectator prompts let you keep supporting the team and can lead to a reinforcement return as a Sheriff's Deputy or Dr. Loomis.

## What Beginners Should Do First

1. Install Patch 1.0.2 or newer. The current patch adds stability fixes and corrects named challenge, achievement, Prestige, interaction, and spectator-XP issues.
2. Play the opening Story Mode chapters to learn Michael's camera, Stalk, Shape Jump, attacks, and environmental interactions.
3. Read the current Passive and Progressive Challenges before choosing a multiplayer role; you may be able to advance several goals in one match.
4. Build a simple Civilian Perk Deck from cards you own. Favor effects you can understand and trigger consistently.
5. In your first Civilian match, identify a working phone, nearby residents, and at least one possible escape before committing to a long objective.
6. Stay until the result screen, even after death, so you can use spectator support and receive completed-match progression.

The full [how-to-play guide](/guides/how-to-play) explains both match loops. This page focuses on decisions that make the first few hours less confusing.

## Start with Story Mode

Story Mode is a six-chapter campaign played as Michael Myers. It retells events around his escape from Smith's Grove and the night he returns to Haddonfield, with narration by Dr. Loomis. Its objectives teach Michael's fundamental movement and hunting tools without putting you immediately against four experienced players.

Optional objectives, collectibles, difficulty settings, and multiple endings give the chapters replay value. Story challenges can unlock alternate outfits, executions, lore, gameplay scenarios, and multiplayer cosmetics. Story Mode also awards XP, so it is useful practice rather than a disconnected cinematic extra.

Story Mode does not teach the complete Civilian side. You still need multiplayer experience to learn resident conversations, team task-sharing, randomized escapes, police calls, and spectator reinforcements.

## Playing as Michael Myers

Michael's job is to terrorize the whole town, not win every chase against a player. Use Killer Sense to locate nearby activity, then Stalk targets to improve tracking and execution opportunities. Kill NPC residents and marked Special Targets efficiently while watching for Civilian activity around phones and escapes.

Shape Jump is your main mobility tool. Enter darkness while unobserved, cross distance unseen, and choose an exit point that gives you an ambush rather than another long walk. Turn off room lights and damage exterior power boxes to create usable routes. Community testing reports a normal Shape Jump recharge of about five seconds after exiting, although a launch bug can leave it unavailable much longer.

Your Michael loadout includes Killer Sense and Shape Jump plus three selected abilities such as Reality Tear, Blackout, or Detection Pulse. Reality Tear can override normal Shape Jump restrictions and stagger nearby Civilians, but community timing currently places its recharge around 3½–4 minutes. Save long-cooldown tools for a defended target, an escape push, or a group that is denying you darkness.

Read the [Michael Myers abilities guide](/characters/michael-myers) for the current official descriptions, cooldown notes, and patch context.

## Playing as Civilians

A Civilian's job is to protect Haddonfield as well as survive. Search houses and points of interest for residents, telephones, tools, weapons, distractions, and escape items. Warn residents through conversation before asking them to hide, follow, or call police.

Split useful jobs without scattering blindly. One player can escort warned residents, another can work an escape, and another can scout a phone or defensive equipment. Communicate when you find Michael, a required item, or an active exit. If you make noise or start an escape sequence, assume Michael may redirect toward you.

Weapons can slow or knock down Michael, but the official rules describe him as unkillable. The Civilian side can instead escape, save residents, or work with police and Dr. Loomis to detain him.

## Finding and Helping Residents

Residents are NPCs living across each map. Search occupied buildings and points of interest, speak to the people you find, and use the available conversation options. Personality affects a Civilian's ability to persuade residents, call authorities, command additional followers, and resist Fear and Stalk.

Do not collect a large group without a plan. More followers can improve rescue efficiency, but careless escorting can create noise and make movement harder. Know whether you are taking them to a discovered escape, telling them to hide, or asking for a police call.

Patch 1.0.1 improved resident pathing into escapes. If a resident still cannot move correctly, try clearing the path or changing approach before abandoning the objective.

## Calling Police

Find a working telephone and complete the current interaction to call the authorities. Resident conversations can enable additional calls, so warning residents is part of the police loop rather than a separate side activity.

Calling police does not instantly end the match. Continue gathering residents, items, and escape progress while officers respond. Once they arrive, cooperate with them rather than assuming AI police can finish every objective alone. The focused [how to call the police guide](/guides/how-to-call-the-police) separates official mechanics from current player observations.

## Escapes

Escape opportunities and required items change between matches. Cars and safe shelters are officially described categories, but no single route or item spawn works everywhere. Explore first, match the item to the objective shown in your current game, and bring residents toward the exit only when it is usable or close to ready.

Starting an escape makes significant noise and reveals its location. Prepare defensive items, alert teammates, and avoid triggering it while everyone else is across the map. Use the [escape guide](/guides/how-to-escape) for current escape types and community-observed sequences.

## Stalk

Stalk belongs to Michael and accumulates per target. Killer Sense helps find victims; keeping one in view builds their Stalk level and improves tracking and execution options. Civilians should break sight lines, use buildings and cover, and avoid standing exposed while Michael watches from darkness.

For Michael, stalking is setup. Use it while targets are unaware or while Shape Jump recharges. Community players report faster progress against injured or frightened targets, but exact rates are not officially published and may change with patches.

## Shape Jump

Shape Jump makes Michael unseen and faster through dark areas. Direct light and observation restrict normal entry and exit, while Shape Dash helps him cross the last stretch. Civilians can keep lights on and watch likely exit points; Michael can flip switches or damage power to take those safe spaces away.

If Shape Jump remains locked far beyond its short normal cooldown, move into clear darkness and break line of sight before assuming it is bugged. Persistent multi-minute lockouts have been reported by players and should not be confused with the intended recharge.

## Perk Decks

See [Perks, Cards and Decks](/perks) for rolling, temporary upgrades, deck selection, and current card changes.

Civilians earn Perk Points from completed matches and use them to roll Perk Cards. Spending more points improves the chance of higher-rarity cards. Owned cards can also be temporarily upgraded for a set number of matches, then arranged into a Perk Deck equipped to a Civilian loadout.

For an early deck, choose cards that support the task you actually perform: movement for scouting, Personality-related help for resident work, Resourcefulness for repairs and vehicles, or Capability for a more defensive role. Card balance can change, so a reliable personal trigger is more useful than copying a launch-day tier list you cannot activate.

## What to Do After Dying

Stay in the match. Complete the spectator prompts shown on screen and use support cards when they can help the remaining team. Current community-tested behavior links these prompts to XP and card rewards, including a chance for a police reinforcement card.

If the return option appears, activate it to re-enter as a Sheriff's Deputy. Dr. Loomis is also an officially confirmed return role, but the developer has not published a guaranteed method for choosing one role or forcing the card to appear. See [how to respawn as police](/guides/how-to-respawn-as-police) for the step-by-step flow.

## Choosing an Early Character

There is no evidence-backed universal best beginner Civilian yet. Each character has four stat categories — Athleticism, Personality, Resourcefulness, and Capability — plus a signature trait and available starting items. Pick a character whose visible stat spread supports the job you want to learn.

Athleticism helps movement and bikes; Personality supports resident and police interactions; Resourcefulness supports repairs, awareness, vehicles, and firearms; Capability supports health and melee or throwable combat. Use the [characters guide](/characters) to compare verified availability and roles without treating cosmetic skins as separate characters.

## Common Beginner Mistakes

- Leaving immediately after death and missing spectator support or a possible reinforcement return.
- Treating the four player Civilians as the entire objective and ignoring NPC residents.
- Chasing one skilled player as Michael while Special Targets and residents escape elsewhere.
- Starting a loud escape before teammates and residents are ready.
- Trying to use Shape Jump in bright areas or while a Civilian is watching.
- Hoarding Perk Points without building a usable deck, or spending everything on temporary upgrades before learning the card.
- Assuming police can kill Michael; the confirmed opposing end state is detention, not death.
- Memorizing exact spawns in a game with randomized items and escape opportunities.
