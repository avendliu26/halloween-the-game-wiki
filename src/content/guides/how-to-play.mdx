In Halloween: The Game, one player becomes Michael Myers while four players cooperate as Civilians. Michael hunts the town's residents; Civilians warn those residents, help them escape, gather equipment and bring police into the fight.

Singleplayer is a separate story told from Michael's perspective. This guide explains the confirmed systems from official material checked September 3, 2026; it does not claim hands-on testing, provide button bindings or promise a working escape recipe for every match.

## Core Match Structure

The [official multiplayer overview](https://halloweengame.com/news/multiplayer-gameplay-overview/) describes a living town with playable characters and non-player residents. The four Civilian slots are not the entire population Michael can encounter.

A useful way to understand a match is as two competing objectives: Michael tries to hunt the residents, while the Civilian side tries to warn and rescue them. This is broader than four people simply running to an exit. As the threat spreads and police become involved, the confrontation changes.

| Side | Main responsibility | Confirmed supporting systems |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Michael Myers | Stalk and kill residents | Special Targets, Killer Sense, Stalk, Shape Jump, Shape Dash, lighting disruption |
| Civilians | Warn and rescue residents while surviving | Conversations, scavenging, changing escape opportunities, police contact |
| NPC residents | React to the threat and player interactions | Hiding, following players and calling police |
| Police / return roles | Help oppose Michael | Patrol escalation, potential deputy or Loomis return opportunities |

## Rescue residents, not just yourself

As a Civilian, look for residents and interact with them to establish the threat. The developer describes a conversation system through which players can ask residents to hide, accompany them or contact police. These are confirmed categories of interaction, not a list of verified button prompts.

Rescue requires both finding people and helping them reach an escape opportunity. An unaware resident and a player already fleeing Michael are in different situations. The game deliberately places those different reactions in the same neighborhood.

The four player-controlled Civilians also have different traits, stats and personalities. The [character directory](/characters) records the confirmed roster; this guide does not recommend a statistical best pick without an evidence-backed comparison.

## Explore, scavenge and reassess exits

The official overview describes points of interest containing loot containers, hiding places, telephones, residents and possible escapes. Loot includes weapons, distractions and key items. Those categories establish what exploration is for without identifying a guaranteed item in every building.

Escape opportunities and the items needed to activate them change from match to match. Treat a familiar landmark as a place to investigate, not a promise of a fixed key spawn or a permanent winning route. When an escape is triggered, the developer says it makes noise that reveals the exact location.

That trade-off is central to the confirmed design: progressing toward an escape can also draw Michael's attention. The overview does not establish a universal step-by-step repair sequence or a best moment to trigger an exit, so we do not present one as tested advice.

## Stealth applies to both sides

The [official stealth discussion](https://halloweengame.com/news/stealth-stalking-and-scares/) describes excessive noise, careless flashlight use and careless resident escorting as ways to attract an unwanted encounter. Remaining undetected is therefore not exclusively Michael's concern.

This is an explanation of the developer's systems, not a guarantee that staying quiet makes a player safe. The exact detection ranges, sound propagation and hiding-place rules are outside the verified evidence used here.

## Understand Michael's toolkit

Michael's Killer Sense helps him perceive nearby victims, while sustained attention builds Stalk for tracking and execution options. Shape Jump is described as unseen travel through shadows with increased movement speed; Shape Dash adds a forward lunge. He can also interrupt lighting and power.

His starting Special Targets offer larger rewards if eliminated within a timer. The overview does not specify the numbers needed to turn this into a precise timing or points guide.

The [Michael Myers reference](/characters/michael-myers) explains each named system and a wording difference between official descriptions of Shape Jump's light/visibility restrictions. Read that detail before assuming an ability can always be entered or left wherever Michael stands.

## Police, death and possible return roles

Civilians can contact the authorities and work with police as the situation escalates. The official overview says a dead Civilian player may return as a sheriff's deputy or Dr. Loomis. It does not promise an immediate return after every death or supply a complete eligibility checklist.

Michael cannot be killed in the described multiplayer setup, but he can be detained and sent back to Smith's Grove. This creates a distinction between defeating his immediate threat and permanently killing the character. No verified detention recipe or damage threshold is supplied here.

## Maps and Single-Player

The [official map announcement](https://halloweengame.com/news/the-locations-of-halloween-the-game/) names four launch multiplayer neighborhoods. Use the [Maps directory](/locations) to distinguish rural East Haddonfield, residential Heights, Orange Grove Estates and Town Center.

The [singleplayer reveal](https://halloweengame.com/news/unleash-hell-upon-haddonfield/) describes six Michael-led chapters narrated by Dr. Loomis. Core chapter objectives are fixed, while the approach is sandbox-driven. Optional challenges, collectibles and difficulty options add replay goals, including cosmetic unlocks for multiplayer.

That story mode should not be treated as a solo version of every multiplayer rescue objective. The sources describe different structures, and this page does not invent a campaign walkthrough from the multiplayer rules.

There is official storefront evidence for the feature, but the reviewed material does not establish every platform pairing, invitation method or cross-progression rule.
